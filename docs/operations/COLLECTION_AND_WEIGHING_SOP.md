# Collection and Weighing SOP

## Before departure
1. Confirm unique collection ID, site ID, country, time window, approved material streams, operator role and transport.
2. Confirm documented permission/access and destination capacity for each stream. If either is missing, do not collect.
3. Check containers, labels, suitable PPE, emergency arrangements, scale condition and offline recording method.
4. Do not promise collection of unapproved material types.

## At the site
1. Confirm site identity and permission. Record arrival time and responsible role; avoid unnecessary personal data.
2. Inspect from a safe position. Stop and keep people away if sharp, chemical, clinical, battery, pressurised, hot, leaking, contaminated or unknown material appears. Do not sort it by hand; follow the incident procedure.
3. Separate material by agreed type and batch. Give each batch a unique ID. Preserve parent-child links if splitting or combining batches.
4. Record event time, collection/site IDs, country, material type, responsible role, batch ID and screening result.
5. Use an appropriate scale on a stable surface. Check zero/tare and operation according to its instructions. Record scale ID, method and evidence reference.
6. Record gross weight (container plus material), tare weight (empty container), and calculate **net weight = gross weight − tare weight**. Subtract tare once only. Net weight must not be negative.
7. Prefer kg for weighed material. Convert g to kg by dividing by 1,000 and record the conversion. Do not convert pieces, litres, bags or volume to mass without a documented material-specific basis. Never add unlike units.
8. If no suitable scale is available, record an estimate only with method and reason; mark estimated/unverified and arrange a verified measurement later. Never claim it as measured weight.
9. Record contamination/excluded items separately. Do not deduct assumed contamination percentages without labelling them as estimates.
10. Obtain site acknowledgement where practical. Acknowledgement confirms the collection interaction, not recycling or downstream acceptance.

## Close the visit
- Check for duplicate collection/batch IDs and required fields, dates, units and arithmetic.
- Record actual end time, material events, discrepancy status and evidence references.
- If digital entry is unavailable, use the offline fallback in the master SOP, secure the paper record, and independently compare later entry.
- Do not mark a planned visit completed if no collection occurred. Record failed, cancelled or partial visits honestly.
- Escalate discrepancies, unsafe material, missing permission or disputed quantities.

## Record linkage
The existing collections register records the visit; material events record each distinct lifecycle event. Do not put multiple lifecycle stages in one quantity field. Use the handover checklist for destination handovers and link records with stable IDs.

## Acceptance rule
Collection is complete as a collection event when the visit and actual collected material are recorded. This does not mean sorting, partner acceptance, reuse or recycling has occurred.
