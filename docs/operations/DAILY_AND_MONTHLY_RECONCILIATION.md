# Daily and Monthly Reconciliation

## Daily close
1. Compare planned visits with actual outcomes; distinguish completed, partial, cancelled and failed collections.
2. Check completed collections have unique IDs, site/country, event time, responsible role and linked material events.
3. Check batch IDs, material types, quantities, units, measurement method and evidence references.
4. Compare custody movements with prior/next custodian and location.
5. Reconcile offered, accepted, rejected and unresolved handover quantities in consistent units.
6. Confirm rejected/unresolved loads have a safe documented location and follow-up owner.
7. Reconcile stock from opening stock, additions, documented removals and actual closing count/weight.
8. Review exceptions, deadlines and stop/restart approvals.
9. Link incurred costs/revenue to operational activity where appropriate; check evidence and currency.
10. Record missing evidence and assign owner/date. Never silently convert blanks to zero or close unresolved issues.

## Material balance
**Expected closing stock = opening stock + additions − documented removals.**

Compare expected closing stock with actual measured/count closing stock. Record the difference, method, investigation owner and resolution evidence. Do not force records to balance by inventing losses or altering old entries. Transfers between locations are outgoing at one location and incoming at another; organization-wide they are not a new collection or external removal.

Do not add sequential lifecycle quantities (collected + sorted + accepted + recycled). They may describe the same physical material. Report stages separately with period and evidence basis.

## Handover reconciliation
For one offered load in a common unit:
- accepted ≥ 0 and rejected ≥ 0;
- accepted + rejected must not exceed offered without documented conversion or destination explanation;
- offered − accepted − rejected remains unresolved until explained;
- a difference is not automatically loss, recycling or disposal.

A receipt supports only what it states. Acceptance does not prove downstream recycling.

## Financial reconciliation
Use Stage 1 finance ledger as the operational transaction register unless a later implementation explicitly selects another single source of truth. Stage 2 trackers are planning/analysis tools; do not duplicate transactions in both trackers and the ledger. Reconcile costs to invoices/receipts and payments. Separate proposed, expected, invoiced, contracted and actually received amounts. An invoice or expected payment is not cash received.

Keep Botswana rows in BWP and Eswatini rows in SZL. Never sum the currencies. Any future conversion must record source, date, rate, formula and original amount; report original currency values too.

## Monthly review
1. Freeze reporting period (YYYY-MM) and country/site scope.
2. Check completeness and duplicates across collections, events, handovers, exceptions, costs and revenue.
3. Report collected, sorted, offered, accepted, rejected, reused, recycled, stock remaining, disposed and unresolved quantities separately with units.
4. Include only evidence-supported reuse/recycling in confirmed totals; show unverified outcomes separately.
5. Report costs/revenue separately and actual cash received separately, by currency.
6. List missing evidence, estimates, corrections, unresolved discrepancies and limitations.
7. Have pilot lead and independent reviewer (where available) approve; record roles, dates and exceptions.
8. Preserve report version and source references. Corrections after approval require a new version and explanation.

## Quality checks
- Unique IDs and valid references.
- No negative physical quantities unless a defined correction event permits them.
- Units consistent or conversions documented.
- Material balance differences have exception IDs.
- Accepted quantities have destination evidence.
- Reuse/recycling claims have outcome-specific evidence.
- Financial records have country/currency and support.
- Actual cash receipts are evidenced.
- Every open high/critical exception has an owner and next action.
