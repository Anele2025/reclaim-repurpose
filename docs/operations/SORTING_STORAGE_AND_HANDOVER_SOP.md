# Sorting, Storage and Handover SOP

## Batch identity and lineage
Create a unique batch ID for each separately tracked material lot. Record source collection, country, material, unit, measurement method and custodian/location. When splitting a batch, assign child IDs and retain the parent ID; when combining, list all source batches. Never reuse IDs or erase lineage.

## Sorting
1. Confirm sorting is within approved scope and location.
2. Inspect safely and use suitable PPE/tools. Do not hand-sort unknown, hazardous, clinical, chemical or contaminated waste.
3. Record input quantity/unit, output streams, contamination/reject quantity, method, time and responsible role.
4. Keep measured loss separate from estimated loss. If input/output do not reconcile, record unresolved difference; do not invent yield.
5. Store streams separately in labelled suitable containers and record location/custodian.

## Storage and stock control
- Use only a suitable approved location with permission, weather/fire/security controls and safe stacking.
- Record every addition, removal, transfer, split, combination, rejection and disposal as a movement event.
- Record opening stock, additions, documented removals and physical closing count/weight.
- Protect material from contamination and unauthorized access.
- If material is damaged, lost, wet, contaminated or inaccessible, record the condition and open an exception.

## Transport and custody
Before dispatch, reconfirm destination, acceptance criteria, opening hours, packaging, quantity limits, fees, transport suitability and required evidence. If conditions change, stop and reconfirm. Record dispatch time, batch IDs, offered quantity/unit, releasing role, transport reference and expected destination. At transfer, record receiving role and time. Custody transfer is not proof of acceptance or processing.

## Destination handover
Record each material stream separately in the handover checklist. Use the same unit for offered, accepted and rejected quantities whenever possible. Reconcile accepted + rejected against offered; any difference remains unresolved. If units differ, retain original measurements and document conversion basis. Retain a receipt, weigh ticket, signed acknowledgement, destination record or suitable alternative evidence; keep confidential documents outside the repository.

For partial acceptance, record accepted and rejected amounts separately. Document where rejected material goes next and verify a safe, permitted route before moving it. Do not abandon rejected loads.

## Downstream outcomes
Record collection, sorting, offered, accepted, reused, recycled, returned, stored and disposal as separate lifecycle events. These stages may describe the same material; never sum them to calculate total recovered material.

- **Accepted:** evidence shows destination accepted the stated quantity.
- **Reused:** evidence supports actual reuse under the reporting definition.
- **Recycled:** evidence supports processing/recycling by the relevant facility or downstream operator.
- **Unresolved:** quantity or evidence remains incomplete.

Acceptance, sale or collection alone does not prove recycling. If downstream evidence is unavailable, report the outcome as unknown/unverified.

## Evidence hierarchy
Prefer verifiable processing records, destination-issued tickets/receipts tied to a batch, signed handover documents, then contemporaneous logs/photos as supporting evidence. Verbal claims and estimates remain unverified. Document exactly what the evidence does and does not support.
