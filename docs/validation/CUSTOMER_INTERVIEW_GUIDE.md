# Customer Interview Guide

Use this guide for exploratory research, not sales pressure. Ask about current behaviour before presenting a proposed service. Do not promise collection, payment, environmental impact, or service availability. Record only notes the respondent agrees may be recorded. Keep names, phone numbers and confidential notes outside the public repository.

## Opening and consent
“I'm researching how [type of site/household/business] currently handles recyclable materials and what practical alternatives might work. This is exploratory; no service is being promised today. May I take brief notes? You can skip any question or stop at any time.”

Record consent status in the interview register. If consent is declined, do not record identifiable interview notes.

## Core neutral questions
1. What happens to the relevant materials today, from the moment they are discarded?
2. Which materials are generated, and how do you know the approximate quantity?
3. What is the unit, measurement period and basis for that estimate?
4. How often are materials available? Is supply seasonal or irregular?
5. Who currently collects, buys or removes them? What works and what does not?
6. What costs, income, staff time, space or inconvenience are involved?
7. What happens when material is wet, mixed, contaminated or not collected?
8. What constraints would prevent a different arrangement?
9. What alternatives would you choose if this service did not exist?
10. What evidence could verify the quantity, current cost or existing arrangement?
11. What would need to be true for you to change your current process?
12. What is the next concrete step you would be willing to take, if any?

## Household path
- How do you currently separate or dispose of cans, bottles, glass, paper or plastic?
- Where do these materials accumulate, and how long can they safely remain there?
- Would collection create inconvenience, storage or hygiene concerns?
- Would you use an existing municipal or informal option instead? Why?
- If a fee were proposed, what would you compare it against? A hypothetical answer is not a commitment.

## Institution or student-residence path
- Who controls access, storage, collection schedules and waste contracts?
- What approvals would be needed from management, facilities, residence leadership or the property owner?
- Are there existing waste contractors, service agreements or policies?
- Who bears costs and who can authorize a new service?
- What volume records exist, and can they be reviewed with permission?
- Would a pilot disrupt safety, cleaning, traffic, security or normal operations?

## Business path
- Which material streams are generated and who currently removes them?
- Are materials sold, returned or collected under existing agreements?
- What storage, quality, timing and documentation requirements apply?
- Who approves a new supplier or collection arrangement?
- What price, service fee or operational benefit would be necessary to justify switching? Ask for actual procurement criteria rather than an invented willingness-to-pay figure.

## Potential collection-site path
- Who owns or manages the site and who can authorize access?
- What activity is permitted there today?
- Are there restrictions on sorting, temporary storage, vehicles, hours or public access?
- Which safety and environmental controls are required?
- Where would rejected material go?
- Can the site manager provide written permission or identify the authority who can?

## Questions that may disprove the model
- What makes the current arrangement preferable?
- What would make you refuse this service?
- Is the material already committed to another collector or buyer?
- Would time, access and storage requirements outweigh the benefit?
- Is the expected quantity too small or inconsistent?
- Who would pay, from which budget, and under what approval process?
- What would make you stop participating after one collection?

## Classifying outcomes
Use the weakest accurate classification:
1. **GENERAL_INTEREST** — positive or curious response only.
2. **STATED_WILLINGNESS_TO_PAY** — respondent says they might pay; no concrete offer.
3. **CONDITIONAL_OFFER** — a specific price/service proposal depends on stated conditions.
4. **DOCUMENTED_COMMITMENT** — attributable written agreement or order, subject to its terms and approvals.
5. **PAID_TRANSACTION** — payment actually received and supported by transaction evidence.

These labels do not substitute for a contract review or accounting records. A documented commitment is not cash received. Never record a sale solely because a person expressed interest.

## Close
“Is there anything important I have missed? May I contact you again about these specific questions? Is there a public or approved contact route I should use?”

Record next actions only where appropriate. Do not enter private contact details into public CSVs.
