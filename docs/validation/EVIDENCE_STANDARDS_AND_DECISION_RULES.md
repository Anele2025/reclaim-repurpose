# Evidence Standards and Decision Rules

## Evidence classes
- **UNTESTED_ASSUMPTION**: a hypothesis or model input with no supporting evidence yet.
- **UNVERIFIED_LEAD**: a prospect or source that may be relevant but whose identity, authority or claim has not been checked.
- **SELF_REPORTED**: a respondent's statement recorded with date, method and consent; it may be accurate but is not independently verified.
- **DOCUMENTED_THIRD_PARTY**: an attributable document, email, quotation, specification, official source or written response. Confirm scope, date and limitations.
- **CORROBORATED**: a material claim supported by independent evidence or multiple appropriate sources; explain how sources are independent.
- **OBSERVED_TRANSACTION_OR_RESULT**: an actual event supported by records such as weigh tickets, transfer receipts, invoices, payment evidence or direct observation. Records can still have limitations or errors.

Evidence strength is claim-specific. An official statute can establish that a law exists, but does not alone prove how it applies to a particular site. A partner quotation documents a quoted price at a point in time, not a guaranteed future price.

## Evidence register fields
Give every evidence item a stable ID and link it to related prospect, interview, partner, site or requirement IDs. Record date, source, verification method, quality, limitations, confidentiality and next action. Keep sensitive source documents outside the public repository; use a non-sensitive reference and restrict access.

## Decision rules

### PROCEED
Only proceed to the next controlled readiness step for a defined country/site/material scope when:
- the intended receiving destination and current acceptance conditions are evidenced;
- relevant site access and permissions are documented;
- applicable legal/regulatory questions and safety controls are resolved for the proposed scope;
- transport, storage, rejected-load handling and traceability are workable;
- the economic case uses dated, traceable inputs and an explicitly reviewed downside scenario;
- the pilot lead records who may authorize operations and who can stop work.

PROCEED is not blanket permission to operate outside the documented scope. It is not available while a mandatory gate is unresolved.

### REVISE
Change the service, site, material, frequency, pricing hypothesis or workflow where evidence suggests a better route. Record which assumptions changed and repeat affected checks.

### PAUSE
No physical collection or handling begins while a mandatory permission, legal applicability, safety, destination, transport, storage, or rejected-load question is open. Also pause if evidence is too weak to support a responsible decision.

### STOP
Abandon the current scope when the site refuses access, the material cannot be safely or lawfully handled, no suitable receiving route exists, or conservative economics show no credible path to viability after reasonable revisions. Record the evidence and rationale; do not conceal negative findings.

## Explicit thresholds without fake precision
Do not use arbitrary universal sample sizes, conversion targets or confidence percentages. Stage readiness requires evidence for each mandatory gate, not a particular number of positive interviews. A small convenience sample can inform a pilot hypothesis but cannot establish population demand. One confirmed receiver is only evidence for the exact material, quantity, location and terms it has confirmed.

For each country and proposed site, label every mandatory gate PASS, OPEN or FAIL and cite evidence. Any OPEN or FAIL mandatory gate means no physical operations. A stage documentation PR does not constitute evidence that any gate has passed.

## Integrity and corrections
Retain negative and contradictory evidence. Do not overwrite a failed lead with a positive status without recording new evidence and date. Do not infer a sale from interest, recycling from collection, or impact from an unverified weight. Separate quoted, expected, agreed, invoiced and paid amounts. Correct errors with an audit trail.
