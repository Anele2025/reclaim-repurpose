# Site Access and Permission Guide

## Principle
Permission must match the exact site, activity, material, storage arrangement, dates/hours and people involved. Informal encouragement, silence, access on a previous occasion or permission for one activity is not blanket authorization for another.

## Site-specific process
1. Identify the actual owner, occupier, property manager or authority responsible for the proposed location.
2. Describe the activity precisely: observation/interview, collection, weighing, sorting, temporary storage, vehicle access, transfer or public engagement.
3. Ask which written permission, local-authority approval, institutional approval, contract variation or other authorization is needed.
4. Keep access to private property, student residences, institutional grounds, public spaces, storage locations and receiving facilities as separate permission records.
5. Record request date, responsible office, response, conditions, scope, validity/expiry and evidence reference.
6. If the responsible authority is unclear, record the question and follow-up action; do not assume permission is unnecessary.
7. Confirm any approval covers the people, materials, methods, hours and duration proposed.
8. Check safety, security, transport, hygiene, fire, weather and public-access controls before activity.
9. Reconfirm permission and destination availability before any proposed operation.
10. Keep full letters, names, signatures and confidential correspondence in restricted storage outside this public repository; record a safe reference here.

## Status meanings
- NOT_ASSESSED: nobody has determined the permission position.
- REQUIRED: permission is known to be required but has not been requested.
- REQUESTED: a dated request has been made and a decision is pending.
- APPROVED: evidence documents permission for the stated scope and dates.
- REFUSED: the responsible party refused permission.
- NOT_APPLICABLE: only use with a written rationale and cited source/authority that explains why no permission applies to this exact activity.

An oral response should be recorded as oral evidence and not represented as written approval. Where the legal effect of an approval is uncertain, seek clarification from the issuing authority or a qualified local adviser.

## Stop conditions
Do not collect, sort, store, transport or transfer materials if required permission is refused, absent, expired, out of scope or unresolved. A positive site manager response does not replace a required regulator or local authority approval.
