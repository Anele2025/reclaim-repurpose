# Stage 4 — Customer, Recovery-Partner and Site Validation

**Status:** Validation plan only. No interviews, customer commitments, partner agreements, site approvals, or operating results are asserted by this document.

## Objective and scope
Test whether a narrowly scoped, non-hazardous-material recovery pilot can be accessed safely and operated with a verified receiving destination and defensible economics in Botswana and Eswatini. Keep each country's evidence, currencies, permissions and decisions separate.

Stage 4 builds on the Stage 1 records, Stage 2 planning model and Stage 3 draft operating procedures. It does not authorize collection or replace advice from relevant authorities or qualified local advisers. Do not build an application during this stage.

## Questions to disprove or support
1. Can a specific customer or host site describe a recurring problem and a realistic material stream?
2. Is enough consistently available material present, with manageable contamination and handling?
3. Will a verified receiver accept the exact material, grade, condition and batch size under current terms?
4. Can the site owner and relevant authority approve the proposed access, storage and activity?
5. Can the service cover transport, labour, supplies, rejected material, administration and payment delays under evidence-backed assumptions?
6. Are there simpler alternatives already serving the site better?
7. Can the pilot avoid hazardous/specialist waste and unapproved activity?

## Target respondents and recruitment
Recruit respondents through lawful, respectful, non-coercive approaches: households, student-residence or institutional managers, small businesses, site managers, and potential receiving/recycling organizations. Ask permission before recording interview notes. Do not imply affiliation with a university, municipality, government, or recycler. Do not scrape private contact details or publish them in this repository.

Use purposive exploratory interviews, not a claim of statistically representative sampling. Record who was approached, who declined, and why a prospect may not represent other customers. A small number of interviews can identify failure modes but cannot establish market size.

## Two-week initial schedule
- **Days 1–2:** Select one candidate material stream per country for investigation; review Stage 2 assumptions; prepare questions and evidence IDs.
- **Days 3–5:** Interview prospective generators/hosts and document current disposal practice, quantities, frequency, constraints and alternatives.
- **Days 4–7:** Contact prospective receivers through public or consented channels; ask for current acceptance specifications, minimum quantities, price basis, rejection rules and payment terms.
- **Days 6–9:** Identify the actual site owner/authority for each candidate site; request written clarification on access and activity. Research country-specific requirements independently.
- **Days 8–11:** Obtain dated transport, supplies and handling cost evidence where possible; reconcile estimates with Stage 2 without changing unknown values to zero.
- **Days 12–13:** Cross-check evidence, log contradictions and unresolved questions, and calculate conservative scenarios.
- **Day 14:** Complete the findings report and make a country- and site-specific PROCEED, REVISE, PAUSE or STOP recommendation.

This is a proposed workflow, not a claim that these activities have occurred or will fit every respondent's availability.

## Interview and evidence procedure
1. Assign a stable ID before recording a prospect or interview.
2. Explain the exploratory purpose and obtain consent for notes.
3. Ask neutral questions about current behaviour before describing the proposed service.
4. Record answers as statements by the respondent, not independently verified facts.
5. Separate stated willingness to pay from a specific offer, written commitment and completed payment.
6. Ask receiving partners for exact material specifications and terms; date every quotation and note expiry.
7. Keep private contact details and confidential evidence outside the public repository. Store a non-sensitive evidence reference here.
8. Record refusals, contradictory evidence and negative findings.
9. Link every material financial input to its source, date, units, country and confidence.
10. Never collect, store, sort or transport materials until relevant permission, safety, legal and destination gates are resolved.

## Evidence quality
Use the classifications in [Evidence standards and decision rules](EVIDENCE_STANDARDS_AND_DECISION_RULES.md). Evidence quality must describe what was actually observed or documented. An official source does not automatically resolve whether a particular activity requires permission.

## Country-specific work
Maintain separate records for Botswana and Eswatini. Do not transfer a price, permit interpretation, transport estimate, customer statement or partner acceptance from one country to the other without new evidence. Use BWP for Botswana and SZL for Eswatini unless a documented transaction specifies otherwise.

## Stage 4 pass criteria
Stage 4 is ready for a decision review, not automatically ready for operations, when:
- The proposed material, target site and service boundaries are specifically described for each country under consideration.
- At least one prospective generator/host and one prospective receiver have been investigated with dated evidence; this does not imply a representative sample.
- The receiving party's material acceptance conditions and current commercial terms are documented or explicitly marked unresolved.
- Site access and permission questions have a named responsible authority/contact and evidence of response or a documented unresolved action.
- Safety and country-specific mandatory gates are resolved for the proposed scope, or the decision is PAUSE.
- Stage 2 inputs are linked to evidence or remain explicitly labelled assumptions.
- Contradictory and negative evidence is included in the findings report.
- A decision and outstanding actions are recorded separately for each country/site.

A documentation PR cannot pass market validation by itself. If evidence is unavailable, report the gap honestly.

## Decision boundaries
- **PROCEED:** only to the next controlled readiness step for a specifically defined scope when mandatory gates are evidenced as satisfied.
- **REVISE:** change material, site, service model, schedule or pricing hypothesis and repeat affected checks.
- **PAUSE:** do not begin physical operations while a mandatory permission, safety, destination, or legal question is open.
- **STOP:** abandon the current model/scope where evidence shows no workable route, unacceptable risk, or economics that cannot realistically be repaired.
